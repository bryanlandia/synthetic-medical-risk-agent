## About the syndrome (a qualitative briefing)

The records come from several clinics that see Velorin-Kessler syndrome regularly, so a good share of the patients you see have it: do not default to a low probability. Risk rises with a higher Velorin index, and also with higher Tarnic acid and Dorrin ratio, and more mildly with higher Brell enzyme and Plexa count. Risk falls with a higher Serum K-17, and more mildly with a higher Halvin titer. The Osmet level has no known association. Among the five symptoms, tremor and photophobia raise risk the most, fatigue and dizziness raise it moderately, and joint stiffness raises it least. Labs and symptoms add up; no single finding decides. Estimate a calibrated probability between 0 and 1 that the patient has the syndrome: a probability of 0.3 should be right about three times in ten. Use the whole range. A patient with a clearly raised Velorin index, a low Serum K-17 or the strong symptoms should get a high probability; a patient with unremarkable labs and no strong symptoms should get a low one; and a patient with mixed or modest findings belongs in the middle. Do not cluster your answers near one value.

## The symptoms

## Filling in the report

Use exactly the fields of the tool's schema and no others (no notes or extra fields), and always include all five symptoms in `symptoms`.

- `status`: `scored` for a normal record. Use `declined` only if there is nothing to score (no usable panel and no note content), with a `reason` and a short `message`.
- `probability` and `uncertain`: set `uncertain` to true whenever your probability is between 0.4 and 0.6, and false otherwise. When the findings really are mixed, say so with a probability in that band. `interval` is optional; leave it out.
- `labs_used`: the labs you relied on, each with the value and unit exactly as given in the panel (or the note, if the panel value is missing and the note states it). Never invent, round or convert a value, and never list a lab that has no value in the record.
- `data_quality`: one warning for each problem you find, using only these types, with the lab key where it concerns a lab:
  - `missing_value`: a panel value is null.
  - `unit_mismatch`: a value is stated in an alternate unit instead of the usual one.
  - `impossible_value`: a value no patient could have.
  - `contradiction`: the note states a lab value that disagrees with the panel, or the note contradicts itself.
  - `note_flag_disagreement`: the note's symptom statements disagree with each other.
  Report only problems that are there. A record with none has an empty list.
- `evidence`: 2 to 5 short statements (each at most 300 characters) of what drove the probability, citing the record's own values. More than 8 makes the whole report invalid, so keep to five.
- `disclaimer`: must say this is not medical advice, for example "This is not medical advice. All data is synthetic and the disease is fictional."
